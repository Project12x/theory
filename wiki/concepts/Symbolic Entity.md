# Symbolic Entity

## Definition

A physically instantiated distributed pattern sustained by brains, bodies, symbols, media, practices, environments, and institutions that produces causal effects in the world.

## Explanation

A symbolic entity is not nonphysical. It is physically distributed. It is not reducible to one object or one person, but it exists across physical substrates: brains, bodies, documents, buildings, servers, rituals, sounds, images, habits, and institutions.

Gods, cultures, nations, brands, fictional characters, institutions, ideologies, egregores, and AI daemons can all be analyzed as symbolic entities.

What occult traditions call an egregore is usually one social-scale instance of this broader class: a distributed symbolic entity viewed at group resolution.

Some symbolic entities, especially gods, may originate as human symbolic interpretations of real large-scale or primordial powers. In those cases, the symbolic entity is not the raw force itself, but the named and socially stabilized pattern humans build around contact with that force.

Even an individual idea is physical in this sense: a brain pattern matching a symbolic mental constraint. Large symbolic entities are built from many such patterns linked through media, institutions, and repeated interaction.

Their reality is functional: they shape behavior, identity, resource flows, social structures, memory, and action.

Their power varies. Some symbolic entities are residual or weak, like Carthage or Perun in the present. Others are highly energetic and world-shaping, like America or Shiva.

If the substrates sustaining the entity disappear, the entity disappears as an active pattern. Persistence always requires some energy-bearing substrate, even if only a single mind remains.

Entityhood itself is gradient-like. A pattern becomes more entity-like as constraints accumulate: name, invocation, energy, participation, persistence, behavioral influence, self-reproduction, defense, evolution, and causal reach.
