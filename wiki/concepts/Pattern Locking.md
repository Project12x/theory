# Pattern Locking

## Definition

The process by which a mind stabilizes its constrained symbolic world-model through repeated social and environmental reinforcement.

## Explanation

A conscious world-model is not merely created once. It locks into patterns through ongoing participation in shared reality, language, society, and feedback. Complete separation from society can break or destabilize this pattern-locking process.

The self is constantly changed by instincts filtered through observation and classification, internal symbol arrangement, and social pressure. Pattern locking stabilizes the self-pattern; [[Pattern Breaking]] changes or destabilizes it.

Society is a major pattern-locking medium. Names, roles, rituals, recognition, obligations, and shared categories help stabilize selfhood.

Practice is the individual version of this mechanism. Repetition energetically feeds a brain pattern, making the symbolic constraint easier to invoke and the physical neural relation more stable.
