# Metaverse

## Definition

The larger structure in which multiple universes, black-hole structures, unconstrained substrate regions, and information-boundary-separated realities can exist.

## Explanation

"Multiverse" suggests many universes, but "metaverse" better captures the intended ontology: a larger level that contains or relates universe-like structures without assuming they share ordinary space.

Something beyond our universe's pure information boundary does not exist in our universe, but may exist in the metaverse.

If separate lattices do not interact, they cannot be said to meaningfully exist in the same space. Their separation is not distance inside a shared container; it is absence of shared relation.

The metaverse should not be imagined as an outside space into which universes expand. There is no outside the universe besides infinite pure possibility. Expansion is actualization into itself.

Between separate universe-lattices is analog unquantized potential. It is not a finite spatial gap. It is infinite relative to the quantized relation-steps of any actualized lattice, which is why universes cannot touch.

As an image, the metaverse is a bit like a foamy gel that expands infinitely. This same foamy/gel-like structure is reflected at scale within the universe itself, but any given universe remains bounded by its cosmic horizon. The image is useful as long as it is not mistaken for ordinary material foam inside shared space.

The "foam" language operates at multiple levels in this system. The metaverse is one foam level, but not the only one: there is also quantum-scale foam and universe-scale cosmic foam. See [[Foam Across Scales]].

This is not the consumer-tech meaning of metaverse. It means the broader ontological structure beyond any single universe's relational information boundary.

The "foamy gel" description is a structural metaphor, not a claim that the metaverse is literally a chemical gel.
