# Empirical Magic

## Definition

Symbolic manipulation that produces observable effects in physical reality.

## Explanation

Empirical magic names the continuity between ancient symbolic practice and modern technology, science, engineering, art, law, mathematics, and AI. The claim is not that supernatural fantasy works. The claim is that symbolic constraint and manipulation can produce real effects.

The refined mechanism is internal first: consciousness constrains reality into manipulable meta-symbolic patterns, then uses those patterns to act on the world. Physical symbols stabilize and transmit these constraint structures, but they are not the deepest layer.

Technology is one expression of empirical magic: natural powers are understood, constrained, and made repeatable. What fantasy calls magitech is structurally close to ordinary civilization once the supernatural costume is removed.

Historically, empirical magic did not vanish when modern science arrived. The successful parts of older magical practice became modern disciplines; the failed or unfalsifiable parts were left behind under the label magic or superstition.

## Notes

The term is powerful but rhetorically risky. It may need alternate names for academic, technical, or public-facing contexts. Avoid reducing it to "arranging physical symbols"; the core operation is constraint and pattern manipulation.
