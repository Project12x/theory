# Tone Combination Can Return To White Noise

## Example

Combining tones can return a listener to white noise.

## Why It Matters

This gives a sonic example of the resolution-limit curve. Constraint initially creates musical meaning by selecting tones, intervals, rhythms, and structures from broad sonic possibility. But if enough tones are combined densely enough, the result can stop functioning as distinct musical structure and become noise again.

## Pattern

The pattern is recursive:

- Too little constraint: undifferentiated sonic possibility or noise.
- Useful constraint: tones, intervals, scales, rhythm, melody, harmony, music.
- Excessive density or over-combination: distinctions blur and return toward noise.

This parallels the broader thesis: constraint extracts meaning until the system crosses a resolution limit, after which meaningful distinctions collapse.
