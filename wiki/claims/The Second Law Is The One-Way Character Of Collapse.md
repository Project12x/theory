# The Second Law Is The One-Way Character Of Collapse

## Claim

The second law of thermodynamics is not fundamentally about disorder increasing. It is about the one-way character of collapse: actualized relational history accumulates irreversibly while usable gradients are spent.

## Reasoning

Under this framework, pure possibility contains no actual information because no relation has yet been established. Once collapse begins, relation appears, information appears, and a causal chain begins to record actualized structure.

Each collapse event narrows what remains possible inside the rendered system. At the same time, exploitable differences are gradually spent as systems settle into more stable low-maintenance relational configurations. Entropy increase is therefore the coarse-grained sign of two linked processes:

- more relational history has been fixed
- less free gradient remains available for work

This is why heat engines fail at equilibrium and why current requires difference across nodes. The system has not become meaningless chaos. It has become too settled to yield further causal leverage without new relation entering the system.

## Implications

- Entropy increase should not be read simply as disorder emerging from order.
- The second law expresses irreversibility of actualization.
- Heat death means exhaustion of usable gradients, not necessarily featureless chaos.
- Thermodynamic arrow and time's arrow are deeply linked in this framework.
