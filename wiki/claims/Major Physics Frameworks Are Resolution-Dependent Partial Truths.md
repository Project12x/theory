# Major Physics Frameworks Are Resolution-Dependent Partial Truths

## Claim

The major frameworks in physics are likely not mutually exclusive falsehoods competing for a single winner, but resolution-dependent partial truths that each capture a real aspect of one deeper mechanism.

## Reasoning

If reality is generated by one deeper relation-forming process, then different successful theories may each be describing that process at different scales, cuts, or projection layers.

General relativity may capture large-scale geometric behavior of rendered relation. Quantum mechanics may capture the discrete and probabilistic appearance of finite relation updates. Hypergraph approaches may capture substrate-adjacent combinatorial structure. Higher-dimensional geometric frameworks may capture aspects of force unification and projection. Relational and structural philosophies may capture the ontological priority of interaction and geometry over isolated objects.

More specifically, GR and QM may both be correct about emergent causality while missing its deeper source. GR captures the large-scale geometry of causal structure. QM captures the finite-resolution, probabilistic, quantized behavior of causal updating. What both lack is an account of why causality has that form at all. See [[Causality Emerges From Pure Causal Possibility]].

Under this view, the frameworks do not fail because they are simply wrong. They fail when they mistake their scale-limited success for a total description of the whole causal chain.

Some approaches may still be poorly thought out or genuinely wrong. But the recurrence of relational, geometric, combinatorial, and structural ideas across multiple frameworks is more likely a sign of partial contact with reality than a coincidence of shared confusion.

## Implications

- Physics should not be read only as a deathmatch between totalizing theories.
- Apparent contradiction may often come from comparing theories built for different resolutions as if they addressed the same layer.
- Unification may require deriving multiple existing theories as scale-limited approximations of one deeper process.
- A framework that cannot recover existing successful theories as limits is weaker than one that can embed them.
- The history of physics may be better understood as layered partial captures of one deeper mechanism.
