# Naming Gives Power At Social Scale

## Claim

The magical idea that giving something a name gives it power is wrong at the small supernatural scale, but the intuition is correct at the social-symbolic scale. Naming can give something power by making it communicable, recognizable, repeatable, and enforceable across minds.

## Reasoning

A name does not grant mystical force by itself. But a name creates a symbolic handle. Once something can be named, it can be transmitted, recognized, invoked, categorized, coordinated around, argued over, protected, attacked, worshipped, legislated, bought, sold, punished, or preserved.

This is more powerful than the old magical framing usually recognized. Naming does not merely affect an isolated object. It can create or stabilize a distributed pattern across society. A name lets many minds point at the same constraint and run related behavior around it.

## Implications

- Some magical intuitions are wrong in scale, not entirely wrong in structure.
- Naming is a tool of institutional formation.
- Social entities gain power when they become nameable and transmissible.
- The danger of naming is not supernatural; it is that named patterns become easier to propagate and coordinate.
