# Cosmic Horizon And Black Hole Horizon Are The Same Boundary

## Claim

The cosmic horizon and the black-hole event horizon are the same kind of boundary viewed from different sides.

## Reasoning

If there is an area of unconstraint, it will become constrained and form a lattice. That lattice expands by actualizing relation into itself, producing a cosmic horizon with an information boundary due to expansion.

A black-hole horizon is the same boundary from the opposite side: the place where rendered relational structure reaches a limit and meets unconstraint or an inaccessible other-side structure. The cosmic horizon and event horizon are therefore not unrelated phenomena. They are the same kind of phase/information boundary under different orientations.

In this stronger reading, a black hole, especially a supermassive one, is the cosmic horizon in inverse. What appears from our side as the self-actualizing frontier of the universe may, from the opposite orientation, be the frontier of a black-hole structure generating space from the inside.

What exists beyond such a pure information boundary is not in our universe, even if it exists in the larger metaverse.

The cosmic horizon is not the edge of an object expanding into pre-existing external space. It is the expansion of actualization. Inverse language about "expanding into space" only makes sense as a description of the other-side orientation of the same boundary, not as motion of our universe through an outside container.

## Implications

- Universe formation and black-hole horizon formation share one structure.
- Horizons are information boundaries produced by relation/unconstraint interfaces.
- Expansion creates a cosmic information boundary analogous to an event horizon.
- Our cosmic frontier may be the inverse orientation of a black-hole frontier.
- The black-hole/event-horizon distinction must be made carefully: the horizon exists in our universe; the black hole itself does not in the ordinary sense.
- Information boundaries define universe membership.
- The cosmic horizon is the expansion of actualization, not expansion into something.
