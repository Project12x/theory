# No Truly Stable State Exists While The Horizon Expands

## Claim

No truly final stable state can exist in an expanding universe-lattice, because the cosmic horizon continually creates new relation in n dimensions.

## Reasoning

The primordial lattice may be the maximally stable low-energy relation-state available within the universe, but it is not absolutely final. The same instability that prevents pure unconstrained substrate from remaining unconstrained also prevents the lattice from becoming eternally superstable.

Why? Because space is not a fixed container. The universe expands by actualizing new relation at its horizon. That means the boundary of the lattice is continually generating fresh relation, fresh constraint, and fresh causal structure. As long as the horizon continues to expand, the system is not closed into a perfectly finished equilibrium.

This prevents absolute terminal stability. A region may relax into lattice-like exhaustion or local heat death, but the larger structure continues creating new relation at the boundary. In that sense, both pure substrate and fully crystallized lattice are unstable extremes: one collapses toward relation, and the other is continually destabilized by the production of further relation.

## Implications

- Heat death should not be treated as absolute final stillness in a globally expanding universe.
- The primordial lattice is a maximally stable accessible phase, not a forever-static endpoint.
- Cosmic expansion is itself a persistent source of destabilization because it keeps producing new relation.
- The universe remains dynamically unfinished as long as horizon actualization continues.

## Revision Or Falsification Conditions

This claim would need revision if the expanding horizon does not create genuinely new relation, if the lattice can remain perfectly stable despite ongoing horizon actualization, or if global expansion can terminate without affecting the possibility of final equilibrium.
