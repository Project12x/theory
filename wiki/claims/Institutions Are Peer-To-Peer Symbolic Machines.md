# Institutions Are Peer-To-Peer Symbolic Machines

## Claim

Institutions are distributed symbolic machines. They are not merely collections of individuals or top-down structures; they persist peer-to-peer through symbolic participation, communication, repetition, and constraint.

## Reasoning

The best evidence for what institutions are is how they are destroyed. If people stop participating in them, stop recognizing their roles, stop repeating their rituals, stop obeying their permissions and punishments, and stop communicating their symbolic patterns, the institution dissolves.

An institution exists only while its pattern is actively run across people. Society itself is this kind of peer-to-peer symbolic machine. It persists because people continually transmit and enact its names, roles, categories, rules, stories, obligations, and rituals.

Physical artifacts can preserve some degree of the pattern after participation weakens. Art, language, law, architecture, documents, symbols, records, and other physical constraints can store institutional form. But storage is not the same as execution. The institution becomes fully active only when minds re-run the pattern.

Running the pattern consumes literal thermodynamic energy. Institutions require bodies, food, labor, buildings, travel, media, heat, light, money, and time to remain stable.

## Implications

- Institutions are real, but their reality is distributed and enacted.
- Institutional power depends on symbolic recognition and participation.
- Refusal to participate can weaken or destroy institutions.
- Art, language, documents, rituals, and architecture are storage media for institutional patterns.
- Society itself is a peer-to-peer symbolic system, not an object above the people running it.
