# Causality Emerges From Pure Causal Possibility

## Claim

Causality is not fundamental in the absolute sense. It emerges from a medium of pure causal possibility as information begins transferring at `c` through the collapse of patterned structure out of no structure at all.

## Reasoning

In this framework, pure possibility contains no actual information and no rendered structure. Causality as we know it is therefore not simply "already there" as a finished background rule. It appears when collapse begins constraining possibility into relation, and information starts transferring through those relations at a finite rate.

This does not happen after some duration of pre-existence. There is no temporal waiting before collapse begins, because time itself appears only with the causal chain. See [[There Was No Temporal Before The First Relation]].

That finite causal rate is `c`. Once relation is established and transfer becomes ordered, the causal chain exists. Time then appears as the ordered tick of that chain, space as stabilized rendered knot-position, and structured physics as the downstream behavior of emergent causality.

Under this view, both quantum mechanics and general relativity are downstream descriptions of emergent causality. Quantum mechanics captures the finite-resolution, discrete, probabilistic behavior of causal updating. General relativity captures the large-scale geometric organization of causal structure once it has stabilized into rendered spacetime.

Neither framework fully explains why causality has the form it does, because both begin after causality has already emerged from deeper possibility.

## Implications

- Causality is primitive within the universe but emergent in deeper ontology.
- `c` is the operational constant of emergent causality.
- Time is the ordered sequence of causal updating, not an independently fundamental container.
- GR and QM should be read as different resolution-views of emergent causality rather than as rival total descriptions.
- Unification requires going earlier in the causal chain than either GR or QM currently does.

## Revision Or Falsification Conditions

This claim would need revision if causality must be treated as absolutely fundamental rather than emergent, if `c` cannot be coherently read as the operative limit of emergent causal transfer, or if no deeper possibility-to-causality transition can be made conceptually or formally coherent.
