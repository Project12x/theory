# Only The Event Horizon Exists In Our Universe

## Claim

The black hole does not exist in our universe. Only the event horizon does.

## Reasoning

In this framework, the event horizon is the in-universe boundary where our rendered relational structure meets an area of unconstraint or a structure beyond ordinary relation. What observers inside our universe encounter is the horizon: the information boundary, causal boundary, and phase boundary.

The black hole itself is not an object contained inside our spacetime in the ordinary sense. It is the other-side structure of the boundary. From within our universe, only the horizon is part of the rendered relational system.

Something beyond a pure information boundary cannot be said to actually exist in our universe. The black-hole structure may exist in the larger multiverse/metaverse, but not as an in-universe object.

This also means that if our own cosmic horizon is the inverse orientation of a black-hole frontier, what exists "there" is not a separate in-universe region we are moving into. It is the opposite-side structure of the same boundary.

## Implications

- A black hole should not be modeled as simply an object located inside space.
- The event horizon is the physical/informational interface available to our universe.
- The interior is not an interior region in the ordinary in-universe sense.
- Black-hole ontology should be horizon-first.
- In-universe existence is defined by participation in our relational information structure.
- Inverse descriptions of black-hole/cosmic-horizon structure should not be mistaken for ordinary shared space.
